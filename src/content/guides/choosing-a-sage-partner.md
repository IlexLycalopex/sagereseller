## What separates a good partner from a reseller

The software is only half of the decision. Two businesses can buy the same Sage product and have very different results, and the difference is usually the partner.

A good partner will:

- Tell you when a product is not the right fit, even if it costs them the deal.
- Show you projects like yours and let you speak to those customers.
- Name the consultants who will work on your project.
- Be clear about what is fixed in the proposal and what is not.
- Support you after go-live with a team that knows your system.

## Reference questions worth asking

When you speak to a partner's customers, ask:

- Did the project finish on the date and budget agreed at the start? If not, why not?
- Were the consultants you met in the sales process the ones who did the work?
- How did the partner handle a disagreement or a problem?
- What is support like now, and how quickly are issues resolved?
- Would you choose the same partner again?

## Red flags

- A proposal that is much cheaper than the others with no clear reason.
- No customers of your size or industry willing to speak to you.
- A delivery team that cannot be named before contract.
- Vague answers on data migration.
- Support that routes most issues to the vendor.

## Fixed and variable scope

Most proposals mix fixed and variable elements. Fixed-scope approaches, such as pre-configured templates, reduce risk and shorten projects, but need you to accept standard processes where they fit. Variable scope gives more flexibility but more uncertainty on cost and time. Neither is right in every case; what matters is that you know which is which before you sign.
