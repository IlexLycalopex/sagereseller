## Where Business Central is stronger

Microsoft positions Business Central for small and midsize businesses and says it is used by 55,000 companies. It works with Outlook, Excel and Teams, and covers operations in the core product: manufacturing with detailed bills of materials, assembly to order and production orders; warehousing with pick and put-away supported by barcode scanners; and AI-powered sales and inventory forecasting.

Its prices are published, which makes early budgeting straightforward.

## Where Sage X3 is stronger

Sage X3 supports process as well as discrete manufacturing. Sage lists advanced warehouse management, manufacturing planning and end-to-end batch genealogy, and lot and serial numbers can be tracked to their final destination with expiry dates calculated as required. Sage describes Sage X3 Warehousing as a configurable, rules-based warehouse management system for medium-sized manufacturers and distributors.

Deployment is broader too: SaaS, private cloud or on-premise. See <MysoftLink to="mysoft-industry-distribution" position="body">Sage X3 for distributors at Mysoft</MysoftLink> for examples.

## Cost and implementation

Business Central's per-user prices are published; Sage X3 is quoted. Mysoft publishes its <MysoftLink to="mysoft-methods" position="body">Sage X3 delivery options</MysoftLink>, from 80 service days over six months (excluding manufacturing) to 140 service days over twelve months. Compare five-year costs using the [ERP total cost of ownership guide](/guides/erp-tco/).
