## Where Business Central is stronger

Business Central is a strong fit for smaller distributors and light manufacturers. Purchasing, stock, sales and basic production sit in the core product, and its native links to Outlook, Excel and Teams make it familiar from day one.

Microsoft publishes per-user pricing by edition and has a very large partner network, so budgeting and partner choice are straightforward. For a business with simple operations and a Microsoft-first IT estate, it is often the sensible choice.

Business Central is a sensible choice when:

- Your manufacturing is light or assembly-based.
- You run one or two sites with straightforward stock.
- Microsoft 365 alignment is a priority for IT.

## Where Sage X3 is stronger

Sage X3 is built for operational complexity. Process and discrete manufacturing, MRP, multi-site warehousing and end-to-end lot, serial and expiry traceability are in the core product, rather than supplied by extensions from several publishers.

That matters over time. Each extension adds its own licence, support contract and upgrade dependency. Businesses that have grown through Business Central often find that the extensions, not the core product, drive cost and risk. Sage X3 keeps those processes in one system with one upgrade path.

Deployment choice is broader too: cloud, private hosting or on-premise. See <MysoftLink to="mysoft-industry-distribution" position="body">Sage X3 for distributors at Mysoft</MysoftLink> for examples of the processes it covers.

## Cost and implementation

Business Central's published per-user pricing makes the licence easy to estimate. For a complex operation, add the extensions, their support and the integration work before comparing.
