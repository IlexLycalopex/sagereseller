## Where Business Central is stronger

Business Central's clearest advantage is its place in the Microsoft ecosystem. Teams that live in Outlook, Excel and Teams get native links between the ERP and the tools they already use, and the Power Platform gives IT teams a familiar way to build workflows and reports.

It also covers more operational ground for small and lower mid-market businesses. Inventory, purchasing, warehousing basics and light manufacturing are in the core product, so a distributor or small manufacturer can run operations and finance in one place.

Microsoft publishes per-user pricing by edition, and there is a large partner network, which makes early budgeting and partner choice straightforward.

## Where Sage Intacct is stronger

Sage Intacct is designed around the finance function. Its multi-entity consolidation and dimensional general ledger are the reason many growing groups choose it: you can report across entities, departments, projects or funds without exporting to spreadsheets, and add a new entity without restructuring the chart of accounts.

For services firms, SaaS businesses and non-profits, Sage Intacct's project accounting, contract revenue and fund reporting are common reasons for selection. Mysoft sets out <MysoftLink to="mysoft-industry-services" position="body">how Sage Intacct works for professional services</MysoftLink> in more detail. Its audit trail and controls are built for finance teams that answer to auditors and boards.

If your stock is simple and your complexity is financial, Sage Intacct usually gives the finance team more control with less customisation.

## Cost and implementation

Business Central's published per-user prices make the licence easy to estimate, but extensions and implementation often make up most of the first-year cost. Sage Intacct is priced on a quote basis. Ask both suppliers to cost the same scope over five years, including support. Our [Sage Intacct cost guide](/guides/sage-intacct-cost-uk/) sets out the drivers.
