## Where Business Central is stronger

Business Central is built into the Microsoft ecosystem. Microsoft describes it as working with the Microsoft 365 applications you already use, such as Outlook, Excel and Teams, to reduce data entry and switching between applications.

It also covers operations in the core product. Microsoft lists manufacturing with detailed bills of materials, assembly to order and production orders; warehousing with pick and put-away supported by barcode scanners; and AI-powered sales and inventory forecasting. Microsoft positions it for small and midsize businesses and says it is used by 55,000 companies.

Its prices are published, which makes early budgeting straightforward.

## Where Sage Intacct is stronger

Sage Intacct is financial management software first. Sage describes it as automating multi-entity consolidation across currencies, from two entities to hundreds. Subscription billing supports more than 500 subscription, usage and hybrid billing models, with revenue recognition to ASC 606 and IFRS 15, and project accounting is included.

Sage has added AI agents to Sage Intacct, including AP automation with vendor and purchase order matching and dimension coding, and a financial intelligence agent that answers questions in plain language.

For services businesses, Mysoft explains <MysoftLink to="mysoft-industry-services" position="body">how Sage Intacct works for professional services</MysoftLink>.

## Cost and implementation

Business Central's per-user prices are published; Sage Intacct is quoted on modules, users, entities and contract term. In both cases, implementation and any extensions or add-ons are extra. Compare the same scope over five years using the [ERP total cost of ownership guide](/guides/erp-tco/).
