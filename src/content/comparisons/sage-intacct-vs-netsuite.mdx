## Where NetSuite is stronger

NetSuite's main advantage is breadth in one product. Finance, CRM, inventory, order management and ecommerce sit in a single suite from one vendor, so a business with complex order-to-cash processes can run most of them without building integrations.

It also has a large global footprint. Groups with subsidiaries in many countries will find a wide partner network and an established OneWorld edition for multi-subsidiary accounting.

NetSuite is a sensible choice when:

- Stock, fulfilment and ecommerce are as important to you as the finance function.
- You want one vendor accountable for most of your operational systems.
- Another part of your group already runs NetSuite and you want a common platform.

## Where Sage Intacct is stronger

Sage Intacct is built finance-first. Its dimensional general ledger lets you tag transactions by entity, department, location, project, customer or fund, and report across any combination without a separate reporting tool. For multi-entity groups this usually means a faster close and less spreadsheet consolidation.

Its open API and marketplace suit businesses that prefer best-of-breed tools: keep the CRM, billing or ecommerce platform your teams already use and connect it to a strong financial core.

In the UK, Sage Intacct is delivered by Sage partners who implement and support it locally. For many mid-market finance teams, a partner that knows UK tax, audit and reporting practice is a practical advantage. You can read <MysoftLink to="mysoft-intacct-implementation" position="body">how Mysoft implements Sage Intacct</MysoftLink> to see what that looks like.

Sage Intacct is a sensible choice when:

- Your complexity is in entities, dimensions, revenue or funds rather than in stock.
- You want finance to own the system, with operational tools connected around it.
- You value a UK-based delivery and support team.

## Cost and implementation

Neither vendor publishes UK list prices, so avoid comparing headline quotes. Ask both suppliers to price the same scope: modules, entities, users and integrations. Then build a five-year view that includes implementation, renewals, support and any add-ons. Our [Sage Intacct cost guide](/guides/sage-intacct-cost-uk/) sets out the main cost drivers, and the [ERP total cost of ownership guide](/guides/erp-tco/) shows how to build the comparison.
