## Where Rillet is stronger

Rillet positions itself as an AI-native ERP for venture-funded startups, focused on core accounting: general ledger, month-end close, receivables and payables, revenue recognition and reporting.

Its focus is automation for SaaS. Rillet states that it generates revenue schedules and books ASC 606 entries for flat-rate, usage-based and milestone pricing from contract data synced from Salesforce or HubSpot, and that its Aura AI checks for errors, suggests and posts entries, and flags exceptions during the close. Rillet also claims that its AI agents automate more than 93% of manual accounting functions; treat that as the vendor's claim and test it on your own data.

## Where Sage Intacct is stronger

Sage Intacct covers more than SaaS revenue. It includes project accounting as well as subscription billing across more than 500 subscription, usage and hybrid billing models, with revenue recognition to ASC 606 and IFRS 15. It automates multi-entity consolidation across currencies.

For UK entities, Sage Intacct submits VAT returns to HMRC through Sage Regulatory Reporting. The Sage Intacct Marketplace lists more than 350 integration partners, and Sage has added AI agents for AP automation and financial questions.

## Cost and implementation

Sage Intacct is quoted on modules, users, entities and contract term; ask Rillet for a quote on the same scope. Factor in the cost of change too: a system that has to be replaced after an acquisition or a move into the UK means a second implementation. See the [ERP total cost of ownership guide](/guides/erp-tco/).
